## What QuickBooks Online is

*[Watch from 5:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0aXgVoVybNU&t=350s)*

QuickBooks Online is an online cloud accounting solution. If you run a business, you need your books kept, and this is a way to digitally keep all of your financials organized in one place. All your transactions go in, and out come reports like a profit and loss report, so you can see whether your business is profitable, or a balance sheet, which shows your assets, your debts and liabilities, and your equity. Put good information in and you get quality information out.

It does much more than that. You can:

- make bookkeeping very easy
- invoice customers and keep track of them
- track cash flow, outgoing bills and who you owe money to
- track projects and project profitability
- run payroll
- keep track of sales tax and file 1099s for contractors
- keep track of mileage
- manage your chart of accounts and reconcile your bank accounts

Some people ask why they should pay for software that seems like just compliance software. But you can't afford not to have accounting software. It's far more expensive to miss a tax filing, and if you aren't keeping meticulous track of your business expenses, you could miss out on a lot of tax savings. So I encourage everybody to get into an accounting system and make sure everything related to the business is included in it.

## Bank feeds: the lifeblood of the system

*[Watch from 8:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0aXgVoVybNU&t=534s)*

If you do one thing every single month, make sure your bank accounts are connected and that you add the transactions brought in from the bank feeds. QuickBooks Online connects directly to your bank: you set up a checking account, choose link account, and it pulls the transactions for you. It has read-only access, so it can see that you received or paid money but can't do anything to your account. Then, as the user, you put each transaction in the right account, the right bucket, and tell the system what it was for.

This is the lifeblood of the accounting system. Link everything you use in your business, such as checking, savings and credit cards. If this part isn't being done, you won't get any reports out of the system, because this is how it finds out what is going into income and where the expenses are.

In the example I walked through, a transaction looked like an equipment rental. I found an equipment rental expense account, and it turned out to be a job expense, perhaps a deposit we got back. QuickBooks Online is pretty smart: if you categorize something with a similar description, it makes a recommendation in green. When I'm actually doing the bookkeeping, I look at receipts so I know exactly what a transaction is before I add it. There's also a matching concept, where we go line by line and figure out where each item needs to go.

I'll cover how to use the bank feeds in a separate video. Classifying these transactions is the bread and butter of the accounting system.

## Invoices

*[Watch from 12:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0aXgVoVybNU&t=735s)*

If you send invoices, I love them to be connected to QuickBooks Online. If you use a separate system, such as a CRM or something specific to your industry, you can sometimes link it in so you can see it in both places.

The great thing about having invoices in QuickBooks is that an accounts receivable report shows at any point in time how much is outstanding. If you need to start collecting, it's right there in your accounting system, and it's accurate. When money comes in, QuickBooks can recognize that, for example, 477 dollars and 50 cents matches an incoming payment, which makes it easier to match the payment and close out the open invoice.

Invoice activity also shows whether an invoice was opened, viewed and paid. If customers aren't getting back to you, you can see when they've actually viewed the emailed invoice.

## Vendors and expenses

*[Watch from 13:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0aXgVoVybNU&t=836s)*

You can keep track of all your vendors in QuickBooks Online and add a new one at any point, with their company name, address and even their EIN. If you need to track payments to a vendor for 1099 purposes, you can check a box, which makes 1099 reporting much easier at the end of the year. Open a vendor and you can see all the transactions related to them, which helps you stay organized.

This works for reporting too. A business owner who wants to know how much they paid their bookkeeper over the year can look that vendor up and find all the transactions. Or if you want to see how much you're using one vendor versus another, you can, as long as the bookkeeping is being done properly.

## Reporting

*[Watch from 15:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0aXgVoVybNU&t=912s)*

Reporting is one of the reasons I love QuickBooks the most, because it's really dynamic. My favorites are the balance sheet and the profit and loss every single month, plus accounts receivable if invoicing is kept in QuickBooks Online. There are lots of other ways to look at the P&L, accounts receivable, accounts payable, inventory and payroll. When the bookkeeping is done properly, you can dissect your numbers and learn almost anything you want to know.

One report I love is sales by product and service. It shows which invoices went into which type of work, so you can analyze your information and get more business insights.

## Garbage in, garbage out

*[Watch from 16:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0aXgVoVybNU&t=979s)*

The concept behind any accounting system is garbage in, garbage out. If you don't put information in a way that lets you track it, you won't get great reporting out. So in the bank feeds, I always tell people to make sure there's something in the description and something in the payee. I want the payee to say Books by Bessie, because then when I go to that vendor I can see all the transactions that say Books by Bessie. If I don't take the time to fill out the vendor or customer, I won't have that great reporting capability.
