> **TL;DR:** A full month of bookkeeping in QuickBooks Online comes down to a few steps. First, make sure every bank and credit card transaction has been pulled in from the bank feed, then categorize each one, reviewing what QuickBooks guesses and adding vendors. Next, reconcile each account against its statement using the statement ending date and balance. Finally, review the profit and loss and balance sheet for anything strange, and use the books review to see what else is left. Do a full balance sheet reconciliation at least once a year.

## Making sure the bank transactions are in

*[Watch from 2:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=136s)*

When I'm thinking about starting the bookkeeping for a month, the first thing I want to do is make sure my bank transactions have been pulled in. That means everything from my credit cards and all my different bank accounts. I want all of that information in my accounting system so I can properly classify it into the right buckets, or accounts.

To get there, click on the accounting app and then go to bank transactions. That takes you to the bank feed page. I'm going to minimize the menu on the side to give myself more room.

Up at the top, the tiles represent the different accounts we have, and we have multiple accounts to reconcile here. The number in the gray box indicates how many transactions have been pulled in from the bank feed and still need to be coded. The tile also tells you the last time the account was refreshed. In this case, 25 minutes ago the system checked the Chase account for new transactions, so it's pretty up to date.

I'm recording this in August, and we're going to work on finishing the July books. So I'm not that worried about August transactions, but I'll look at the July ones and code all of them. If I happen to do some August transactions along the way, I just get a head start on next month.

## Coding the main checking account

*[Watch from 4:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=265s)*

I don't really care which account I do first. Some people like to start with the main checking account, so let's start there. There aren't a lot of transactions. You can toggle the date to show newest first or oldest first, and I'm going with oldest first.

The first one is office rent going to Landlord Larry. Those little stars mean QuickBooks has recognized the transaction and thinks it knows the category, in this case rent. I agree with that, because that is what it is. I make sure the toggle is set to categorize, not match. If it's on match, it will try to find an existing transaction to match the expense to, and that's not what we want. Then I post it.

- **Owner's draw:** This is money going out, and it goes to owner's distribution. That's correct.
- **Transfer to business savings:** This one is paired, because the business savings account is also in QuickBooks, so it recognizes money going out of one account and into another. I do think that in this new version they're trying to help avoid the duplicate transactions that used to happen a lot with transfers between accounts. That's my hope, anyway.
- **Facilities:** This one is cell phone, which is correct, so I post it.
- **Rent again:** There's another rent charge because we had two months of transactions, so I just went ahead and did these August transactions as well.
- **Another distribution:** Same treatment as the first.
- **Credit card payments:** These are the Chase credit card payments. They're both paired to the Chase credit card, since that's also connected to the bank feed.
- **Income:** Some income came in, which is AdSense revenue.

QuickBooks is doing a pretty good job recognizing things and putting them in the right place, and now the main checking account is done.

## Savings and the credit cards

*[Watch from 6:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=384s)*

Next is the savings account. There's some interest being earned, so I make sure it goes to the right account, which is interest income. Again, the little star means QuickBooks has chosen an account for us, so we always need to review those. We don't want to just guess. This bank account is for Relay, and we're coding it to where the money came from and posting.

Then there are additional items on the credit card. Here's 99designs. We can always add vendors, and I highly recommend, as best practice, always having a vendor or customer name on any expense or any income, because that helps you have better reporting. So I save a vendor name there. This is one where QuickBooks did not quite get it right. I'd say it's more of a marketing expense, though since this is a sample company I'm making my best guess on some of these, and I went with professional development.

Going down the list:

- A resort travel charge, which was automatically set to hotel. I kept hotel.
- A water delivery, which I'm calling office kitchen.
- A software charge, where a rule was actually already set up, so that one's good.
- Ziply is internet, and Hiscox is insurance. You can always add a new vendor.
- Kit is marketing software, so software is correct.
- Parking.
- Meals.

This is also where the 1099 question comes up. If you think you've paid a vendor who might need a 1099, such as a sole proprietor or a single-member LLC, ideally before you pay any vendor you get their W-9 and find out whether you need to track their payments for 1099s. You can easily set that up in the vendor setup.

Once those credit card transactions were coded, we had the business savings, the checking and the other credit card done. There's no activity in some of the other smaller bank accounts, so all the bank accounts and credit cards are up to date. It says some pending transactions haven't pulled in yet, but since we're doing the prior month, I consider this ready to reconcile to the bank accounts and statements.

## Reconciling to the statements

*[Watch from 9:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=586s)*

There are a couple of ways to get to reconcile. One is to go to the bank register, and whatever register you're on (this is the credit card register), you can click reconcile up there.

The last time this credit card was reconciled was through June 9th. It's too early to get the August statement as I record this, so I reconcile through the statement ending date of 7/9/2025, with a statement ending balance of 1847.22. When you look at a statement, it's clear what the ending statement date is. It is not the due date of the bill, because those are two different dates. The statement will show a beginning date and an end date, and then the balance as of the end of the statement date. Those are the two things you need to type in.

What QuickBooks does is select everything that has cleared, and then you can go through your bank statement and check that those items are there. Typically, because you've been pulling transactions in from the bank feed (that's what the little green symbol is), it's very likely to match the bank statement or credit card statement with no problem.

Where we see discrepancies more often is when you start changing things and doing things manually, or you write a lot of checks and some are outstanding and haven't yet cleared. Or if there are errors in your accounting system, like a duplicate transaction for whatever reason, you can go in and clean those up. But this one is nice and clean, pulled in from the bank feed, so it's good to go and I finish it.

Then I do the same with the other accounts: statement ending date, statement ending balance, start reconciling. This next card turned out to be interesting. The statement says American Express credited us for 10 cents, and that was not pulled in from the bank feed for some reason. Maybe it's one of the pending transactions that needs to come in. I can add it right here, and I have to make sure it has the right date, otherwise it won't show up in our reconciliation at the right point in time. This happened on 6/23. Once I add that, we have no issues.

This is why you don't want to wait too long to do your bank reconciliations. You don't want to have six months at a time, because finding a tiny difference in six months of transactions could get really messy. This business doesn't have a lot of transactions, so it's easy to find. But if you have a lot of them, something like this can be a nightmare to sniff out.

You can always go line by line through your bank statement and say, okay, I see this 1435, I see this 330, I see this 140. Really detailed bookkeepers do that, especially when they have a lot of transactions and especially if it's not automatically reconciling. That's the old-school way: you literally go line by line and check things off. QuickBooks just does it a lot faster with a bulk check of everything in the date range, and then you see if there's a difference. If everything was pulled in from the bank feed, it should match the statement.

## Checking the ending balance and the totals

*[Watch from 14:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=865s)*

Now the bank accounts. For the main checking account, I put in the ending balance and start reconciling. Make sure your ending balance is correct, because if you type it in wrong, you'll be looking for a difference that's really just an error you made. For example, if I accidentally typed it in wrong and saved it, it would tell me I have a 98 cent difference. I'd be sitting here looking for a 98 cent difference only to realize I just typed the number wrong, which would waste my time, and we don't want that.

With the right numbers, everything matches up, with no difference to find. You'll also see the beginning balance, minus payments, plus deposits. Often these payments and deposits should match what's on your bank statement. Sometimes different banks present the information differently, like ACH income versus wire income, or different types of deposits, so you might need to do a little math. But these totals can be helpful when you're troubleshooting why the account doesn't match. You might be able to say, well, our deposits match, so it must be on the payment side. That helps when you're auditing and trying to figure out why things aren't reconciling.

I finish that one, then reconcile the last bank account, which didn't have a lot of activity, so that was pretty easy. The other accounts had already been reconciled because they had no activity in them.

## Reviewing the reports

*[Watch from 16:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=981s)*

At this point the bookkeeper's job is kind of done for the month of July. But any good bookkeeper should do a review before marking anything as final. So I go over to reports and look at the profit and loss to see if anything stands out. Maybe something is in the wrong category, got put to uncategorized, or is sitting somewhere it shouldn't be. Depending on the preferences of the users of the financial statements, you might want a high-level review too. I run it for last month.

Then I look through and ask what's going on. Nothing is sticking out as miscategorized, everything looks like it's in good buckets, and I don't have anything in miscellaneous. You could be looking for lots of different things. The role of the bookkeeper is not to comment on why net income is only $56 or anything like that. But as a bookkeeper you might go, wait, that may not be normal, so is there anything I'm missing? Did I refresh the bank feeds? Are we sure everything's there? Well, we already did the bank reconciliation, so yes. But is there anything else I need to consider, and did I do everything I was supposed to do as the bookkeeper? Really it's as simple as that. The P&L looks fine.

I also run a balance sheet: go to reports, then standard reports, and make sure nothing looks very strange there. What's strange in your business will be different from what's strange in the sample company, so I can't tell you exactly what would pop out. What I'm looking for in a bookkeeper review is that nothing is in miscellaneous or uncategorized, and that a new account hasn't been accidentally created by the system. Sometimes QuickBooks will do that, and it doesn't make any sense. Everything is in the proper accounts here, so there's nothing to worry about.

## Using the books review

*[Watch from 18:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=1127s)*

Going back to the dashboard, I expand the accounting app, and we can do a books review. This is like a needs-attention list. It includes transactions without payees, personal transactions, loan payments to review, and cash transactions to record. You can add additional items here too. It's a good way to see if there's anything else that needs to be done.

There are no bank transactions, so that's done. There are no uncategorized transactions, no undeposited funds and no unapplied payments. If you wanted to go in and add payees to transactions without them, that's probably best practice, like I've been saying: it's good to have a vendor or a customer associated with each line.

It also shows some unreconciled things on the credit cards, because we only reconciled up to the last statement date, since the next month's statement was not available. You can see this one is only reconciled through July 11th, and that's through July 9th. That doesn't bother me as long as all of the bank feed transactions have been included. I would still be able to close the books for the month without doing the August reconciliations, but that's my personal preference: I would still review the books, maybe not close them, without having the next statement done. You can also see that some other balance sheet accounts have never been reconciled, and that is something to look at. The books review can help you understand anything else you need to do to wrap up.

## The bare minimum

*[Watch from 20:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cMDoHOG238&t=1249s)*

Overall, what you need to do for a full month of bookkeeping at the very bare minimum is classify and categorize all the transactions in the bank feed, then reconcile the books, and then do an overview of your profit and loss and balance sheet to make sure nothing is weird and wonky.

I also like the idea of doing a full balance sheet reconciliation at least once a year, for taxes for sure. You don't necessarily need to do that every single month unless you have a lot of balance sheet activity.
