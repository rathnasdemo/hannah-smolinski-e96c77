*Adapted from the video [How Inventory Works in QuickBooks Online (QBO)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo)*

> **TL;DR:** QuickBooks Online inventory lives under Sales, in Products and Services. I create an inventory item, with its income, inventory asset and cost of goods sold accounts, then buy more through a bill using item details. The bill increases the inventory asset and accounts payable. Invoicing a customer records the income, and QuickBooks records the cost of goods sold on its own. As volume grows you may need a third-party app, and inventory requires the Plus subscription.

Today we are talking about QuickBooks Online inventory. We will look at how you put items into inventory, how to edit an inventory item, how purchased items get into inventory, and how selling items affects the inventory balance through invoicing.

We take one item through the whole process so you can see how it flows through QuickBooks. That way you can decide whether you need this for your business, another app attached to QuickBooks Online, or whether you want to stay really simple and use a spreadsheet for the few things you have.

## Finding inventory in QuickBooks Online

*[Watch from 1:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=77s)*

I am in the sample company, in the accountant view. If yours does not look like this, you can go to the gear icon and switch to accountant view. To find inventory, go to sales and then products and services. If you are wondering where inventory is, it is all under products and services.

This sample company is a landscape services company, so they do some services and also hardscaping and landscaping, which means they have some inventory. You can tell whether something is a service or inventory by the type column.

## The item types

*[Watch from 2:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=152s)*

When you create a new item, you get these options:

- **Inventory.** Products that you buy or sell and want to keep track of. They affect cost of goods sold: when you sell them, they move out of inventory and go to cost of goods sold.
- **Non-inventory.** Things you buy and want to keep track of, but you will not sell them on a unit for unit basis or track quantities. To be honest, we really do not use this one very often. Most things, if they are physical and you want to keep track of them, are inventory.
- **Service.** Like CFO services, or Design Services for this client. A service is anything that does not have a unit attached to it.
- **Bundle.** If you have maybe three different inventory items you like to sell together, perhaps slightly discounted, you can create a bundle.

## Creating an inventory item

*[Watch from 3:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=232s)*

Let's create a new inventory item for this landscape company: tulip bulbs. Here is what I fill in:

- **Name and SKU.** Maybe there is a numbering system you like to use internally. I follow what they have for their other SKUs.
- **Category.** You can create different categories. They have design, landscaping and pest control, so I put this under landscaping.
- **Quantity on hand and as of date.** This is how many you have when you first create the item. I say 100 on hand as of April 30th.
- **Reorder point.** If you always need to keep some in stock, you can set a level, maybe 20, below which you need to reorder. I usually do not use that, but it is in the system.
- **Inventory account.** Every time you purchase this item, which balance sheet account does it go to? If you have complicated inventory with different inventory assets, you can choose that account. Here we just have one line item, inventory asset.
- **Sales description and price.** The description is what shows up when you make an invoice for a client and sell them tulip bulbs. I put in pink and white tulip bulbs and a price of five dollars.
- **Income account.** This is helpful when you have multiple sales line items and want every sale of that item recorded to the right place on the P&L.

If you need sales tax, you can put in sales tax rates here too.

On the buying side, say you put together a purchase order and send it to your vendor so they can send you an invoice before you pay the bill. The purchase description is what goes on there. Vendors sometimes want to see different descriptions, like an internal code, than what you put on your invoice to your customer. It does not have to be one description for both buying and selling, which I think is really nice.

Then I set the cost at 250, the expense account as cost of goods sold, so every time you buy it, it goes there, and a preferred vendor, Tanya's Nursery. Then save and close. Under landscaping, there are my tulip bulbs, sitting in inventory, and I can see the quantity on hand.

## Selling the item with an invoice

*[Watch from 7:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=457s)*

Let's sell these to Diego Rodriguez. When filling out the invoice, you type in the product or service you are selling, and the description we entered shows up. At five dollars each, he actually wants 10, so his invoice is for fifty dollars. I dated it for today and clicked save and send.

Now let's see what that did in the accounting system. I ran the profit and loss on accrual, because we have not been paid yet and that is the only way it would show up. There is the Diego Rodriguez invoice under sales of product income. If that ends up being the wrong place, I could go back to the individual item, edit the income account, and change it. If we were on cash basis, this invoice would not show up because we have not been paid yet.

## Buying more inventory with a bill

*[Watch from 9:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=584s)*

What happens when you are out of tulip bulbs and need to buy more? There are a couple of ways to do this. Let's add a bill. A quick shortcut is the plus new button in the top left, where you can go straight to bill.

Say you placed an order over the phone with Tanya for 200 more tulip bulbs, and she is sending a bill so you can come in with a check. I choose Tanya's Nursery as the vendor, make it due on receipt, dated April 30th, with Bill 607.

Instead of the category details, you skip down to item details. Most bills, like a utility bill, you just enter as an expense. But since we are doing inventory, we need to use item details. In category details I have no way to search for a product; I can only do that in item details. I find the item, buy 200 of them, and save.

## What the bill does to the books

*[Watch from 11:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=715s)*

On cash basis you would not see this bill on the P&L yet, so I looked at it on an accrual basis. On the balance sheet there are now 200 more tulip bulbs: Tanya's Nursery 500 was added, increasing the balance of the inventory asset. That is the balance sheet side of the transaction.

The other side, until the bill is paid, sits in accounts payable, so it is all on the balance sheet right now. There is Tanya's Nursery for 500, a bill that is unpaid. Once it is paid, the cash goes out and removes the accounts payable.

## The cost of goods sold side

*[Watch from 12:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=777s)*

There is also a P&L side when we sell the item. When we sold the tulips to Diego, we saw the fifty dollars as income. But there are two sides to that transaction: the income and the expense. The cost of goods sold for that transaction is in the cost of goods sold section of the P&L, where we see 10 times 250, because that was the cost of the item, so 25 is recorded.

I did not have to do the cost of goods sold manually. The system does it on its own. Once you create an invoice with inventory items on it, you get the income part and the expense part, and QuickBooks does that for you. On cash basis you would not see either of them, because no cash has traded hands. Once the cash is recorded, you would see the effects.

So that is how inventory flows. You buy goods and add them through a bill or an expense. They sit in inventory until you sell them, and you sell them by invoicing customers. That is a very, very simple overview of the inventory system in QuickBooks Online.

## When you outgrow QuickBooks inventory

*[Watch from 14:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cGiSZ2S4Rzo&t=895s)*

As you get bigger and have a higher volume of transactions, you will probably need something that interfaces with a point of sale system. If you are an e-commerce company, for example, you will not be able to do this manual process for every single sale.

There are apps and tools that interface with QuickBooks Online. Some of them keep the inventory out of QuickBooks altogether and just post bulk journal entries to the key accounts I told you about, like income, cost of goods sold and inventory. I would recommend talking to a QuickBooks expert if you have inventory and are trying to figure out the best way to go, because depending on volume, QuickBooks inventory does have some limitations.

I think it works really well for a company like this that has services and products together and invoices its customers without a huge volume of invoices every month. Other times you might need a third-party app. The great thing is that pretty much every third-party app will interface with QuickBooks Online because it is the biggest software for small business accounting. As your business grows, QuickBooks Online really is the best place to start.
